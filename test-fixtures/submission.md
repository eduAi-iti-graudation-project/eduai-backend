# Persuasive Essay: Powering Our Future

Our city council has the power to shape Springfield's energy future. Right now, our town relies almost entirely on fossil fuels for electricity, contributing to rising local temperatures and poor air quality. Springfield should adopt a community solar program because it reduces carbon emissions, lowers electricity bills for working families, and creates local green jobs.

A community solar program would significantly cut Springfield's carbon footprint. According to the U.S. Department of Energy, community solar installations reduce greenhouse gas emissions by an average of 40% compared to grid electricity (DOE, 2024).

Beyond the environmental benefit, community solar saves families money. The National Renewable Energy Laboratory found that community solar subscribers save an average of $120-$180 per year on their electricity bills (NREL, 2025).

Critics argue solar power is unreliable because the sun doesn't shine at night. However, subscribers remain connected to the grid; the solar array feeds power during peak hours, and the grid supplies power at night.

Finally, a community solar program would create jobs right here in Springfield. A 5-megawatt community solar farm requires roughly 30 construction jobs and 5 permanent maintenance positions.

Springfield has a choice: continue with an outdated, polluting energy system, or take the first step toward a cleaner future. I urge you to vote yes on the community solar pilot program.
